## Summary

Dream Maker is a personal English and Spanish emoji explorer that connects an editable subject to Wikipedia context, artwork, educational videos, GIFs, and sounds. v0.2.0 improves discovery quality, media controls, loading resilience, and the animated portal. It remains a local Next.js application with browser-local favorites and history; public hosting and accounts are not included.

### Progress since v0.1.0

- **Interface and identity:** refined colors, spacing, simpler bilingual text, clearer saved-discovery screens, and a new planet mascot app icon. ([#1](https://github.com/juandarr/emoji-dream-maker/pull/1))
- **Museum artwork:** replaced Chicago image delivery with verified Cleveland Museum of Art and Metropolitan Museum sources. Up to five relevant public-domain works appear in a responsive museum-wall gallery, with attribution, source links, larger-image viewing, fallback previews, and partial-source retries. ([#2](https://github.com/juandarr/emoji-dream-maker/pull/2))
- **YouTube discovery:** added anonymous local yt-dlp metadata search with optional Data API fallback, English/Spanish educational ranking, reusable Python workers, selection prefetch, and a bounded one-hour disk cache. The recorded ten-subject/language sample returned three videos per search with a 1.504-second median uncached response; this is an observation, not a latency guarantee. ([#3](https://github.com/juandarr/emoji-dream-maker/pull/3))
- **Sound curation:** searches concrete scenes and clearly labeled evocative associations, ranks relevant CC0/CC BY previews, removes repeated or misleading matches, and preserves successful scenes when another fails. The recorded 16-subject live metadata review returned three previews for every subject. ([#4](https://github.com/juandarr/emoji-dream-maker/pull/4))
- **GIF selection:** ranks up to 25 candidates for the emoji and selected subject, showing at most three distinct matches. Changed subjects replace the original associations; unrelated metadata, duplicates, missing previews, and known still images are excluded. ([#5](https://github.com/juandarr/emoji-dream-maker/pull/5))
- **Media controls:** artwork supports pointer-centered wheel zoom up to 4×, double-click maximum zoom/fit, drag panning, and arrow-key browsing. Video adds Space playback and F fullscreen shortcuts, including a playback command queued before player initialization. ([#6](https://github.com/juandarr/emoji-dream-maker/pull/6))
- **Performance and recovery:** media begins independently of Wikipedia; gallery code loads on demand, catalog normalization is reused, duplicate requests share work, museum detail slots refill continuously, and partial YouTube failures retain valid lessons. Smaller WebP GIF previews have GIF fallback. Audio coordinates playback and exposes retries. Request/response sizes and saved-data validation were strengthened. ([#7](https://github.com/juandarr/emoji-dream-maker/pull/7))
- **Black-hole interaction:** a rounded orange accretion disk with flowing light strands replaces the earlier center artwork. All three emoji bands have orbit guides. Approaching emojis accelerate the disk, bend into arcs, and create directional blue lensing echoes while tugging the center artwork. Rapid movement builds white-hot energy; slowing, leaving range, cancelling a drag, or closing the portal restores the appropriate cool or steady state. Reduced motion disables agitation and distortion. ([#8](https://github.com/juandarr/emoji-dream-maker/pull/8))

## Interface

Discover combines bilingual search with three orbital lanes around the portal and a selected-emoji panel. Hover/focus makes picking easier; orbit motion pauses during dragging and an open gallery. The new lensing follows the dragged emoji on either side without shifting its grab point. High-frequency movement changes emitted light toward white while preserving the black core; the animation returns to its steady state after the interaction ends.

The gallery preserves the selected discovery while users open artwork or video dialogs. Artwork retains its full composition, loads larger images on demand, and falls back to previews on failure. Viewers restore focus when dismissed through Escape, close, or backdrop. Video opening pauses sound, starting one sound pauses the others, and closing the gallery stops playback and removes embedded video. Phone layouts, English/Spanish controls, and both system and application reduced-motion preferences are covered by browser checks.

## Services and data

| Service | Configuration and state at this version |
| --- | --- |
| Wikimedia / Wikipedia | No API key; configure a compliant server-side `WIKIMEDIA_USER_AGENT`. The recorded configured review returned 22/22 summaries and 6/6 sampled related lookups. Requests share successful metadata and respect pacing, cancellation, and provider retry delays. Rate limits and missing articles remain possible. |
| Cleveland Museum of Art and The Met | No API keys. Searches verify public-domain rights and direct museum image hosts, rank subject evidence, diversify results, and return up to five works without unrelated padding. Real mixed-source previews and larger images were reviewed on desktop and phone. A failing museum does not discard successful results from the other. |
| YouTube via yt-dlp | Requires an installed yt-dlp executable or configured Python archive; supports `auto`, `yt-dlp`, and `api` modes. English/Spanish searches and prepared-card loading were measured with a configured local worker; real embedded playback was also recorded. Flat search metadata cannot establish embedding permission, factual accuracy, or undisclosed AI production. No media downloads or account cookies are used. |
| YouTube Data API | Optional server-side `YOUTUBE_API_KEY` for direct API mode or fallback after an operational yt-dlp failure. Richer restriction checks are implemented; the later full application review used controlled responses rather than a fresh authenticated audit. Provider quotas still apply. |
| Freesound | Requires server-side `FREESOUND_API_KEY`. A recorded credentialed review verified search metadata and attributed previews for 16 subjects; it was not an audio audition. At most three licensed previews are selected, with explicit scene/association labels and partial-search recovery. |
| GIPHY | Requires intentionally public `NEXT_PUBLIC_GIPHY_API_KEY`; G-rated search/media requests remain direct from the browser, uncached and unproxied. Relevance and recovery checks use controlled responses. No live authenticated audit is recorded for this snapshot. Custom ranking/filtering needs provider agreement or a different provider before production use under the limitation documented in README. |

Providers receive subject queries and the normal requests needed to retrieve or play their content. Private credentials remain server-side. Successful server metadata is generally bounded to 256 entries with a one-hour lifetime; complete three-video selections can also persist in the ignored local disk cache. No credential values or contact addresses are included in these notes.

## Limits and planned work

- Relevance selection uses metadata and editable associations, not complete viewing/listening or scientific validation. Empty and partial results remain valid outcomes.
- Provider availability, rate limits, approvals, content changes, autoplay restrictions, and embedding permission remain outside the application's control. Recorded latency samples are not service guarantees.
- GIPHY's custom selection policy limitation remains unresolved for production integration.
- Local video retrieval needs yt-dlp setup; optional API-backed sources need credentials.
- Public hosting, accounts, synchronization, generated media, emoji combinations, and Jev-assisted search remain planned/out of scope.
